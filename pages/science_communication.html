Teaching science as a social process and combating misinformation

Role: Project Manager and User researcher

Timeline: 4 months, 2025

Skills: Lean agile process, project management, user experience design, user experience research

Context

Education scholars often say that one of the reasons we have such a problem with misinformation is that K-12 science classrooms are not teaching students to critically evaluate scientific claims they may encounter in online environments [1]. They point to two remedies for this problem: one is to teach students hard fact-checking skills (e.g., investigating the source of a claim, “lateral reading”, etc.). The other is to teach students that science is a social process [1]. With the information that students are taught currently, students may be led to believe that science is a pure and authoritative source of knowledge, and if scientists have discovered something, then it must definitely be true [1]. However, it seems like educational standards aren’t building in ideas of the importance of scientific consensus, peer review, or publication bias as important factors in evaluating our shared understanding of the world around us [2][3].

Team

5 students: 1 Information Science PhD, 1 Software engineer, 1 architecture student, 2 Masters of Information Sciences students

Problem Space and Needfinding

How might we...

Equip K-12 science educators with accessible methods and resources to teach students that science is a social process built on peer review, consensus, and collective vetting of knowledge claims?

Mixed methods project

  • Compeititve analysis: Explored the problem space looking for competitors to our solutions.
  • Interviews: n=10 with K-12 science educators and english language teachers/admin to understand their current practices and challenges in teaching science as a social process.
  • Affinity Mapping: Visualized pain points and opportunity clusters across the interviews we conducted.

Analyzing the Data

I used Sheets and R to analyze the survey results. I used MAXQDA to extract themes, notably from the Provider side of the interviews.
