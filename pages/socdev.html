Children's Judgments of Dual Identities

A developmental psychology study asking how children judge biracial individuals who present their identity differently depending on context. 303 participants aged 6–13, recruited at a local zoo and online. Findings contributed to an ongoing white paper on child social development.

Problem

The U.S. is increasingly diverse, and people are given the option to claim more than one racial identity. Adults judge biracial individuals who change how they racially identity based on context, known as Contextual Racial Presentation (CRP) negatively.

Kids use race as an ingroup/outgroup signal from surprisingly early ages — "like me" or "not like me" is a calculation children are making well before they can explain it. Biracial people complicate that framework. They belong to multiple groups at once, and sometimes present differently across contexts. What does a six-year-old do with that? What about a thirteen-year-old?

The U.S. is increasingly multiracial, and understanding how these judgments form matter.

Study Design

The study gave children a story about a biracial character. In one condition, the character maintains their biracial identity across contexts. In the other, they shift to a monoracial identity depending on the situation. We then asked children to evaluate the character: fairness, likability, trustworthiness.

The original design was a 2×2 between-subjects structure. It simplified to two conditions based on what the data could actually support. 303 children participated — recruited online and through offsite testing at a local zoo, a deliberately public setting that gets us closer to a general-population sample than a university lab ever could. Fifteen research assistants ran the data collection.

The Role of Identity Misrepresentation and Biracial Conceptualizations in Judgements of Contextual Self-Presentation in the U.S.

Working closely with my graduate student advisor, I helped structure the final paper and navigate the analysis. I also took the lead on writing up the methods and results sections, and I presented a poster at an undergraduate research conference.

Analysis

Analysis ran in R: logistic and linear regressions, chi-squared, Welch's two-sample t-tests. We ran pre-registered analyses first, then exploratory ones to see what else the data suggested. The pre-registration constraint is worth naming — it meant we couldn't just fish for significance. What we found is what we found.

Age shaped responses more than condition in some models, which was itself a finding worth tracking. A six-year-old and a thirteen-year-old are processing social information differently. The developmental arc matters, not just the snapshot.

Methodological improvements for future work

Include diverse racial/ethnic groups in sampling. Explore judgments of both high-status and lower-status monoracial children. Control for and investigate the impact of perceptual features on racial categorization. Examine the role of mutual intentionality in group membership. Study how group approval might influence perceptions of CRP. Investigate how the stability of a participant's own racial identity affects CRP judgments.
